# Give the film its sound

A demo's sound is one voice or one piece of music in front, everything else under it or absent.
Decide what leads, place it, then measure the mix instead of trusting it: you cannot hear the
preview, and a render you did not measure is a guess.

If a tool this skill names is not in your tool list, the installed OpenScreen is older than this
skill: offer to update it (see the `openscreen-demo` skill, step 1), or measure as in
"Without getAudioReport" below.

## 1. Keep it simple

- **One foreground at a time.** Narration leads and the music sits under it, or the music leads
  alone. Never two things competing at full level.
- **Few layers.** A voice and a music bed, or a music bed alone. Sound effects stay minimal: soft
  clicks under the music at most, one impact at most, nothing that doubles a beat the music
  already hits. Layered whooshes, hits and risers turn into noise.
- **Muted autoplay is the default** on X and LinkedIn: the film must work silent, with callouts
  carrying the story. Sound is the bonus, never the only carrier of meaning.

## 2. Narration

**Recorded:** the user narrates while recording (`startRecording` with `microphone: true`, see
`openscreen-demo`), or records a voiceover file you import.

**Synthesised:** use a text-to-speech tool the user already has: a local model, or a service with
their own key. Never sign them up for a paid service. When they have none, a local open-weight
model with a permissive licence for both code and weights is the default (for example Kokoro,
Apache-2.0, without voice cloning). Check the weights' licence apart from the code's: some open
models forbid commercial use.

Write narration for the picture:
- About 2.5 words per second (150 a minute). Count the words against each beat's length before
  generating anything.
- One file per beat or per sentence, so each line can be placed on its beat and moved alone.
- 0.3 to 0.5 s of air before the first word of a beat and after its last.
- Speak what the screen does not already say. Never read the callouts aloud word for word.

Captions for a narrated take belong to the `openscreen-demo` skill: `transcribe`, then
`setCaptionSettings`. Only the voiceover lane is transcribed, so put narration in it.

## 3. Music

**Imported:** a file the user owns or has a licence for. Ask, never assume.

**Composed in code:** synthesise it yourself (a script that writes a WAV) when the user wants
original music and has nothing to import. Its great advantage is a known beat grid:
- At 120 BPM a beat is 0.5 s and a 4/4 bar is 2 s. Beat n falls at n × 60 / BPM seconds.
- Land cuts, zoom starts and the first callout on bar lines; land the result of the key action on
  a downbeat.
- Write a simple progression and a clear pulse; render it at the final length of the film.
- Compose after the picture is locked. Any later change of cuts or speed moves the picture
  against the bed.

**Generated by a model:** only a tool the user has, and only one whose terms allow commercial use
of the output. Free tiers of several music services do not.

A single long generated or composed piece rarely follows the film's arc. Prefer short cues that
change at the story's turns, or one steady bed under a calm demo.

## 4. Bring it in and place it

1. `importMedia` with the file's absolute `path` (and `kind: "audio"` for a sound-only `.webm`).
   It returns the `assetId` and `durationSec`. The file lands in the media, not on the timeline.
2. `addAudio` with that `assetId`, `startSec` (ruler seconds), `endSec` (omit it to play the whole
   file), `kind` (`"voiceover"` or `"music"`), `offsetSec` (where in the file to start),
   `gainDb` (-60 to 12, 0 unchanged), `fadeInMs` / `fadeOutMs` (0 to 5000).
3. `setAudio` with the `audioId` changes any of those, plus `muted` and `loop`, on the whole
   track. `removeModifier` removes a track; `removeMedia` then drops an unused file.

Placement rules:
- Each lane holds one row: two voiceover tracks or two music tracks never overlap in time, while a
  voiceover over a music bed is the normal case.
- A music bed that spans a cut keeps playing its file continuously; it does not restart.
- Music: start on the film's first frame or on the first beat of the story, with a 200 to 500 ms
  fade-in, or none when the file starts on a downbeat. End on a phrase ending, with a fade-out of
  1.5 to 3 s that finishes on the last frame.
- Narration: no fades needed on a clean voice file; start each line on its beat.
- `setEditorSettings` holds the whole-film audio: `audioGainDb` (-12 to 12, the master),
  `recordingMuted` (silences the recording's own sound; added tracks still play), and under
  `cursor`, `clickSound` with `clickSoundGainDb` (-24 to 0) for soft clicks on the recorded
  clicks. Keep clicks well under the music, around -12 dB or lower.

## 5. Duck music under the voice

Music under narration sits 6 to 12 dB below where it plays alone: about 6 dB for a sparse bed,
up to 12 dB for a busy one. Gain is per track, so duck by splitting the bed:

1. Lay the bed as consecutive music tracks of the same file: one before the narration, one under
   it, one after. Each track's `offsetSec` continues the previous one (its `offsetSec` plus its
   length), so the file plays on without a jump.
2. Give the tracks under the voice a `gainDb` 6 to 12 dB lower than the others.
3. Put each change of level 0.3 to 0.5 s before the first word and after the last, on a bar line
   when you know the beat grid, so the step sounds like the music's own phrasing.
4. Do not bring the music back up in a pause shorter than about 2 s: a bed that pumps between
   sentences is the classic amateur tell.

## 6. Loudness targets

| Destination | Integrated | True peak |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | about -14 LUFS (louder is turned down, quieter is never raised) | -1 dBTP |
| X, LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram | no published target: aim about -14 LUFS | -1 dBTP |
| Pull request, README | usually watched muted: about -14 LUFS if it has sound | -1 dBTP |

Under narration, the voice should measure clearly above the music: compare the tracks alone
(below).

## 7. Measure, never trust

`getAudioReport` with the `projectId` measures the sound exactly as the export mixes it. It
returns `durationSec`, `integratedLufs` (null when silent), `truePeakDbtp`, and `silences`, the
spans of at least 0.5 s with no sound.

1. **Whole mix.** `getAudioReport` with `projectId`.
   - `durationSec` matches the film's length.
   - `integratedLufs` within 1 LU of the target. Too quiet or too loud: move `audioGainDb` by the
     difference (it spans -12 to 12 dB), then measure again.
   - `truePeakDbtp` at -1 or below. Over it after raising the level: lower the loudest track's
     `gainDb` instead, or accept a slightly quieter film. Never trade the ceiling for loudness.
   - `silences`: each one is deliberate (an opening before the music, a planned pause) or a bug,
     usually a track that ends early, a gap between narration files, or a fade too long.
2. **One track alone.** `getAudioReport` with `audioId` measures that track by itself. Measure the
   voice and the ducked bed under it: the bed should sit 6 to 12 LU below the voice. A track
   reported silent is muted, empty, or placed outside the film.
3. **After every change of timing** (a cut, a speed region, a moved clip or track), measure again.
   Imported tracks are moved by speed regions, never stretched, so the picture and a bed composed
   on a grid drift apart; re-check that key hits still land with `getFrames` at the beat times.

**Without `getAudioReport`:** `exportAudio` with the `projectId` and an absolute `.wav` path
writes the same mix (`audioId` for one track alone). Then, with `ffmpeg` if the machine has it:
- loudness and true peak: `ffmpeg -i mix.wav -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -` (read `I:` and
  `Peak:` in the summary);
- silences: `ffmpeg -i mix.wav -af silencedetect=noise=-50dB:d=0.5 -f null -`.

## 8. Tells of a careless mix

- Music at full level under a voice; music that pumps up between sentences.
- A film louder than -1 dBTP that clips on the platform, or far quieter than -14 LUFS.
- Sound effects on every click, whooshes on every zoom, an impact on every beat.
- Music that stops dead at the end instead of resolving or fading on the last frame.
- A narration line that runs past its beat, or starts before the picture it describes.
- An unexplained silence in the middle of the film.
